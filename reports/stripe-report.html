Stripe in AI answers

How 6 AI assistants mention Stripe and its competitors when buyers ask 20 questions about billing, fraud, payments, payouts.

Synthetic data. No production answer history was available for this build, so the 14,400 answers behind this report come from the pipeline's synthetic generator, which mimics Bright Data's output and contains planted changes. The method, tables and wording are the real product; the market conclusions are not.

Across 6 assistants, Stripe was named in 81% of 3,105 relevant answers in the last 7 days. It is the most-named brand in 23 of 24 assistant-and-category pairs.

The one real loss is billing on ChatGPT: 84% to 46% from 12 Nov.

Adyen gained on Perplexity (24% to 78% in payouts); Stripe is still named as often there but now appears later in the answer.

Two apparent drops were collection problems, not market moves (Google AI Mode 16–19 Nov; Grok 2–3 Nov). They are excluded from the analysis.

4. How much to trust this

14,400 answers were collected; 94% were real, complete answers. The rest were scraper errors, empty or blocked pages, cut-off answers, or answers to a different question. Only real answers count toward visibility.

AssistantDaysReal answersNormalMain failure
Google AI Mode16–19 Nov 67%95% truncated

These days are excluded from change detection on that assistant. A naive count would have shown visibility collapsing on each of them. A problem that runs to the end of the period is still open: treat that assistant's latest numbers with caution until collection recovers.

Limits. Each assistant was asked 20 fixed questions from logged-out sessions in one country. Real users phrase questions differently, are often logged in, and get personalised answers. Visibility measures whether a brand is named, not whether it is recommended favourably. Positions come from list order and are approximate for answers written as prose.

Appendix: method

Visibility. For a brand, an assistant and a day: real answers to questions in the brand's categories that name the brand, divided by real answers to questions in those categories. With no real answers, visibility is empty rather than zero.

Change detection. For each assistant, category and brand, daily counts are treated as binomial. We pick the single change date that best splits the series (at least four days each side), and compare before and after with a two-proportion test. A change is reported if it is at least 10 points, significant after correcting for every series tested, holds on most days after the change, and shows up on at least two separate questions. High confidence needs a much smaller p-value, three or more questions, and no data problem within two days.

Data problems. A day where an assistant's share of real answers falls below 75%, or 12 points under its usual rate, is treated as a collection problem and excluded.
